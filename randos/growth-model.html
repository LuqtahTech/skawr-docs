Defaults are a realistic base case, not the aggressive target line. Adjust the assumptions to pressure-test your own numbers.

Notes on the calculations

MRR compounding: Active subs each month = prior active subs times (1 minus churn), plus new subs. MRR = active subs times ARPU. This is why SaaS overtakes CRO over time: it stacks, while CRO resets to zero every month.

LTV: ARPU divided by monthly churn gives average customer lifetime revenue. At $175 ARPU and 3% churn, lifetime is ~33 months and gross LTV is ~$5,833. Multiply by gross margin for contribution LTV.

CAC: Total ad spend divided by subscribers attributable to paid channels. CRO revenue is not counted as a cost offset here, so real blended CAC is even lower.

Payback: CAC divided by monthly contribution per sub (ARPU times margin). Under one month means ads pay for themselves almost immediately.

CRO capacity: CRO closes are capped by your delivery capacity each month. Two founders handle roughly 18 concurrent projects. The base case assumes hires at Month 4 and Month 7 to lift the cap.

Two SaaS paths: Sales-assisted subs come through calls (audit to call to CRO to attach) and are capacity-bound. Self-serve subs come from audits that convert to paid without a call (nurture to preview to checkout). Self-serve carries the volume at scale, since you cannot staff ~1,000 calls per month. Only a small slice of audits should book a call; the rest should self-serve.

Upstream volumes: Impressions and clicks are derived from spend and the CPM, CTR, and completion rates. Organic reach is derived backward from the organic audits driver using the reach-to-visit and visit-to-audit rates. Change those rates to see how much reach each audit actually costs.

Boosting: Treat organic posts as free tests. When a post beats the boost threshold on its own, put a small paid budget behind it. You are amplifying proven creative rather than gambling on cold creative, which is the cheapest way to buy reach.
